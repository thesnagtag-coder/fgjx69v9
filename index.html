Before you start - safety first

HSE
Never remove covers, faceplates or panel fronts, and never touch anything that is damaged, scorched, wet or sparking. (Professional practice)
Wet electrics: never switch power or use electrical items while standing in water. A qualified electrician must inspect wet equipment before power is restored. (CDC Electrical)
  • If you see smoke or fire, get out and call your local emergency number. (Professional practice)
  • To check warmth, hold the back of your hand near (not on) a faceplate or plug first. Never touch a damaged or scorched item. (Professional practice)
  • In Pre-Purchase mode you are a visitor: ask before pressing any test button. Tripping a device can cut power to fridges, alarms or medical equipment. (Professional practice)
  • Only look into roof spaces or cupboards where access is safe and permitted. Do not handle visible cables. (Professional practice)
  • All electrical work should be done by a qualified electrician. (NFPA tip sheet, ESFi Home Safety)

Extension Lead Load Check

SHEET 04 · CALCULATOR

Adding up what is plugged into one lead or adaptor is the simplest way to spot overloading. Read the current rating on the lead's label, and the power (watts) on each appliance's rating plate.

Appliances plugged in

Current (A) = power (W) ÷ voltage (V). Use the rating plate, not a guess. UK: most extension leads are rated 13 A, some older leads and many cable reels 10 A or less, and a wound cable reel can be as low as 3-4 A (Electrical Safety First). Other countries: use the rating on your lead's label.

Protective Device Test Log

RCD · GFCI · AFCI

Many homes have devices with a Test button that cut power when a fault is detected (called RCDs in the UK, GFCIs and AFCIs in the US). NFPA advises testing AFCIs and GFCIs once a month according to the manufacturer's recommendations. If a device does not trip or reset, call a qualified electrician.

Pre-Purchase: ask the owner before pressing any test button, or simply record whether devices are present.

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It will not tell you what is wrong with wiring. Warning signs can have several causes, and only a qualified electrician can find them.
    • It never asks you to open, dismantle, test inside or repair anything, or to use test instruments on fixed wiring.
    • It does not replace an electrical inspection or a report required by law in your country.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Universal guidance used in this tool

    • Call a qualified electrician for frequent fuse blowing or breaker tripping, tingling from appliances, discoloured or warm wall outlets, burning or rubbery smells, flickering or dimming lights, and sparks from outlets. (NFPA tip sheet)
    • Warning signs also include buzzing sounds and warm switches. (ESFi Home Safety, ESFi Dos)
    • One heat-producing appliance per outlet at a time. Plug major appliances directly into a wall outlet. (NFPA tip sheet, ESFi Home Safety)
    • Test AFCIs and GFCIs once a month according to the manufacturer's recommendations. (NFPA tip sheet, ESFi Home Safety)
    • Extension cords: do not run under carpets, through doorways or under furniture. A hot cord is a warning sign of overloading. Replace damaged cords. (CPSC Extension Cords)
    • Danger signs: smell of hot plastic or burning, sparks or smoke, scorch marks, damaged or frayed cables, coloured wires showing, melted plastic, fuses or breakers operating for no obvious reason. (ESF Extension Leads)
    • Do not plug an extension lead into another. Fully unwind cable reels. (ESF Extension Leads, ESF Overloading)
    • Consumer unit / panel: look for signs of physical damage, thermal damage or discolouration. (ESF RCDs Explained)
    • Wet electrics: never switch power while standing in water; have a qualified electrician inspect before power is restored. (CDC Electrical)
    • Current: amps (A) = watts (W) ÷ volts (V). A basic electrical relationship used by the lead calculator.

    Country-specific (labelled wherever used)

    • UK: most extension leads are rated 13 A; some older leads and many cable reels 10 A or less; a wound reel can be as low as 3-4 A. If there is no working RCD in the consumer unit, use plug-in RCDs. When buying, ask if the electrics have been fully checked in the past ten years and for a copy of the Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR). (Electrical Safety First)
    • US: GFCI and AFCI devices, monthly testing. (NFPA, ESFi)
    • Everywhere else: check your national electrical safety authority. Voltages, device names, inspection intervals and legal duties differ.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.

      Rules where you live

      The advice in this tool applies everywhere. These notes cover rules that differ between countries. Rules change, so always confirm with your local authority or a registered professional.

      • Emergency numbers: South Africa: 10111 (police), 10177 (ambulance and fire), 112 from a mobile phone. Saudi Arabia: 911 or 999 (police), 997 (ambulance), 998 (civil defence and fire). UAE: 999 (police), 998 (ambulance), 997 (civil defence and fire). UK: 999 or 112. US and Canada: 911. Australia: 000. Save your local number in your phone today.
        Source: Gauteng Tourism - Emergency contacts (South Africa) · Expatica - Emergency numbers in Saudi Arabia (updated Apr 2025) · UAE Government portal - Handling emergencies
      • UAE electricity emergency: Call 991 for electricity emergencies (Abu Dhabi: 800 2332).
        Source: UAE Government portal - Handling emergencies
      • South Africa: electrical Certificate of Compliance (CoC): Every electrical installation must have a valid Certificate of Compliance, issued by a registered electrician. When a home changes ownership, the CoC may not be older than 2 years. Alterations or additions need a new CoC (Electrical Installation Regulations, 2009, regulation 7).

      Where is my information saved?

      Inside this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to The Snag Tag or anyone else. That keeps it private, but it also means you are the only one who can keep a copy safe.

      Why could my information seem to disappear?
      • You open the tool in a different browser, a private/incognito window, or on another device.
      • You open a different copy of the tool (for example a downloaded file instead of your link).
      • Your browsing history or site data gets cleared.
      • Safari (iPhone, iPad and Mac) can delete saved information if you don't open the tool for about 7 days.
      • The tool is open in two tabs at the same time. Always use one tab.
      How do I keep my information safe?
      • Press Save a backup now after every session where you change something.
      • Keep the backup file somewhere safe, like your cloud drive or a USB stick.
      • Always open the tool from the same link, in the same browser.
      How do I get my information back, or use another device?

      Open the tool, press Restore a backup and choose your latest backup file. Everything comes back as it was when you saved it.
